lean-posix: openPty's master is made close-on-exec only after openpty returns, so a fork in another thread can inherit it

Raised in consult round 20261109-155335-a3a0 (argon: descriptor leak audit; sol: descriptor lifecycle audit), checked in the code: `openPty` (`Posix/Pty.lean:14`) calls `openpty` and then `fcntl(m, F_SETFD, FD_CLOEXEC)`; its doc comment promises a master "which no child inherits". A `trySpawn` (fork) or Lean spawn in another thread between the two calls gives the child the master, so the pty never sees EOF while that child lives. The kqueues from `kqOpen`/`openEvents` use the same open-then-fcntl pattern but are not inherited across fork, and the other opens use O_CLOEXEC. Work: open the master atomically close-on-exec (`posix_openpt(O_RDWR | O_NOCTTY | O_CLOEXEC)`, `grantpt`, `unlockpt`, `ptsname_r`, open the slave), keeping the contract; and a test in `lake test` that spawns a child which lists its open descriptors (`/dev/fd`) after `openPty`, `kqOpen`, `lockFile` and `pipe`, asserting that only the documented inherited ones are there. Check: that test, and the existing pty tests.
