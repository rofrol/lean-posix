Signals as kqueue events, a kill of any signal, and a spawn that gives the child the default signals

Asked by rr2 (its issue wq6sdn; the owner approved on 2026-11-09 that rr2 writes its run's end line when stopped by SIGHUP, SIGTERM or SIGINT, and that a lean-posix call it needs is done here first). Lean core cannot catch a signal. Wanted: a call that catches given signals on a kqueue of their own and a blocking wait that gives the next one's number, so a program can wait for them on a thread of its own; signals found ignored at the call stay ignored (nohup, a shell's `&`). Without a C handler (the bindings are inline C expressions, and a Lean function is no `void (*)(int)`): EVFILT_SIGNAL first, then SIG_IGN, as macOS's kqueue(2) says the filter "will record all attempts to deliver a signal to a process, even if the signal has been marked as SIG_IGN". An ignored signal survives exec, so `trySpawn`/`spawn` get an option that puts SIGHUP, SIGINT, SIGQUIT, SIGPIPE and SIGTERM back to their defaults in the child before its exec (only async-signal-safe `sigaction` between fork and exec). And a `kill` of a given signal to a process or group, giving the errno. Tests: a signal sent to the process arrives as its number and does not end it; one ignored at the call is not watched; a child spawned with the option dies of SIGTERM, one without it survives it.

Base it on main (d0d9622) and push a `pin/<sha12>` tag for the result so rr2 can pin it; push nothing else.
