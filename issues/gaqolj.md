lean-posix: termCtl's numeric op as an inductive, signals apart from the screen's ops

`Posix.termCtl` (Posix/Term.lean) takes a `UInt32` `op`: 0, 1005 and 1006 enter the full screen, 1000 leaves it, 1001 installs the signal handlers, 1002/1003/1004 hand the terminal across exec, and any other number is a signal arriving (so 2 cannot be an op, as its doc comment notes). The contract lives only in the doc comment; a caller can pass a number that means nothing intended. Proposal: a public `TermOp` inductive (enter with a mouse mode, leave, install handlers, export/take/drop the handed terminal) decoded to the private numbers in one place, with a theorem that the encoding is injective and never hits a catchable signal number; the exported C handler keeps its numeric entry. Public contract change: rr2 calls `termCtl` (its viewer), and every consumer that calls it moves its pin in its own queue. Needs the owner's approval (AGENTS.project.md, "Types for lifecycles and invariants").

Why (consult round 20261109-170703-dda6, sol and argon, checked on 2026-11-09): rr2/Rr2/Viewer.lean:397-402 and lean-tui/Tui/Term.lean:29-50 repeat the numbers 0 and 1000-1004; a typo becomes a signal dispatched in C. No wrong number is in use today, and lean-tui already wraps the numbers in named functions, so the gain is preventing a future mistake, not fixing a bug. Both models: do it separately from k3dley's return types; argon would drop the injectivity theorem as self-evident for a small match.
